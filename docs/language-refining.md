# Purpose

This document serves to highlight potential issues with tooltips, subheadings, and other prose present in the app.

## Help Tour

**Page 1 of 9**

> SPHEREx is a NASA telescope that photographs the whole sky in 102 colors of infrared light. This page shows every 
> public SPHEREx image of any place in the sky as a short film, so you can see what moves and what changes. The tour takes about a minute.

102 **bands* is more correct, closer to the terminology used by NASA on the Space Apps site.

**Page 2 of 9**

> Type a name such as Vega or Jupiter, or coordinates (RA and Dec). The list under the box offers NASA's five test targets. The tour will use Epsilon Indi B, which you have open.

"Which is already open", not "you have open"

**Page 3 of 9**

> The map went there. The yellow square is the area being studied. Drag inside the square to move it, drag its edge to resize it, or click anywhere on the map to load a new area. Close on its card ends the study.

"The map went there" is ambiguous and means nothing to the user. Use clearer terminology.
"Close on its card ends the study" -> "Selecting close ends the study."

**Page 4 of 9**

> These are the SPHEREx images of that area. Each one is taken in a different color of infrared light. The yellow marks show where the target should be. Click any ring to learn about that object.

Again, "different color" -> "different band" of infared light.

**Page 5 of 9**

> Play them like a film

> Press Play, or step one image at a time. "By visit" adds up each visit, so faint things show and moving things step from place to place. "Deepest" adds up everything.

Don't like "Play them like a film" should be technical wording.

**Page 6 of 9**

> Blink swaps the SPHEREx image with one that an earlier telescope took many years before. Anything that jumps between the two has moved. Try it on this target after the tour.

**Page 7 of 9**

Fine.

**Page 8 of 9**

> Graphs shows the spectrum, which tells what something is made of. Studies finds everything in the field that moved or changed brightness, with one button. Telescopes shows where SPHEREx is right now. Save and share has pictures, films, data files, and links.

"Something", should be "space object" or other more correct terminology. 

**Page 9 of 9**

> Settings holds the detectors, brightness, color, and what is drawn on the map. Help brings this tour back at any time. That is the whole page. Enjoy the sky.

"That is the whole page" is unnecessary.

## General Rules

- All prose and text within the application should be written as technical, while still remaining accessible for all ages and levels of experience.
    - use technical words. Do not use things like "something", "colors", "a thing". Use "objects", "bands", "wavelengths", and other scientific and technical words. Remember, this is a product for educators, scientists, researchers, institutions, and curious technically-minded people (that may be interested in space).
- A final verification pass to ensure all language used is American Standard English 
- Reduce/remove the "sales pitch"-esque wording. 
- Action words should not be in a tutorial
- Short and sweet is better than verbose and lengthy so long as the intended meaning and impact of the prose is delivered
- Do a final pass for vocabulary and grammar choices.
