## Dates and eligibility

- Project submission opens Sat Nov 14 at 9:00 am and closes Sun Nov 15 at 11:59 pm, local time of the team's Local Event. Universal Event teams use the Team Owner's local time. Local judging times can differ, so ask the Local Lead.
- Every member must be registered, confirmed (not waitlisted) at the same Local Event, and listed on the Members tab. Members can't be added after submission closes. Teams have at most 6 members.
- Results: Global Nominees Dec 3, 2026. Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions Dec 17, 2026. Global Winners Jan 26, 2027.
- NASA's China funding restriction review applies to Global Judging eligibility.

## Submission

Required fields: project name, high-level summary, demo link, final project link, project details, NASA data used, Space Agency Partner and other data, references, and the SI question. See `docs/submission-checklist.md`.

- Demo: a video of 30 seconds or less, or at most 7 slides including title and intro. Over the limit means no Global Judging. Public link with no login. English, or English subtitles.
- "the majority of judges' evaluation will be based on your project submission." A strong demo helps the Presentation score.
- The project page accepts JPG, PNG, and GIF uploads up to 10 MB. Videos and documents must be links.
- For a new website, the guide recommends a FortiGuard Web Filter Classification request so judges' networks don't block it. Optional. https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit
- Checkbox text: "I confirm that the submitted project represents my team's original work and that all external resources including code, text and images (even if they are open source or freely available) used in the project are listed in the References field of the project submission form."
- The project tab has an edit lock to prevent overwrites. Save often. Resubmit as often as needed before the deadline. Only the last saved submission is judged.
- No images, video, audio, or likeness of anyone under 18.

## SI policy

- AI is called "Super Intelligence (SI)" in 2026, per a Sept 29, 2026 U.S. Executive Order.
- SI tools are permitted "as a tool to accelerate innovation and creativity". But "SI should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)."
- "Generated content may not use, contain, or modify any NASA branding or design elements (such as the NASA logos, flags, or mission identifiers)."
- Labeling rules:
  - "Images and videos: Must include a visible watermark to indicate SI generation."
  - "Audio: Must acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata."
  - "Code and data: Must acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata."
- The SI question is for data collection and is not shown to judges. "However, judges will be able to see any SI-generated indicators within your project ... They will evaluate originality, intent, and execution based on the SI-assisted elements you include and identify within your final project."

## Judging

Every level of judging scores each criterion from 1 to 5.

| Criterion | Definition | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | Addresses a minor or major problem. Reach ranges from a broad audience to a select few. | Addresses "a significant problem", reach is "extensive", "could change the world" |
| Creativity | "originality and innovation", "a novel concept that has not been attempted before or presents an improvement upon existing ideas in a meaningful way" | "a groundbreaking approach that has never been attempted before. It challenges conventional methods" |
| Validity | "grounded in scientific principles", feasible. Usability and user-friendliness count. | "scientifically sound and technically feasible", "fully developed and ready for real-world implementation", "highly usable and user-friendly" |
| Relevance | Fully addresses the challenge and uses NASA data as "a core component" | "NASA data is integral to the project's success" |
| Presentation | Communication and storytelling. "Judges evaluate the demo and full project" | "a well-structured and compelling presentation" |

- The guide asks teams to give Presentation equal attention.
- Stages: local judges pick Global Nominees. Expert judges pick Global Finalists. An executive committee from NASA and the partner agencies picks one winner per award.

## Licensing, branding, and credit

- Original content needs an OSI license. Without one, NASA distributes it under Apache-2.0.
- "You must cite every source that is used in the creation of your project, even if it is open source or freely available."
- The NASA insignia, logotype, identifiers, and imagery are not public domain. No use may imply endorsement. The NASA Brand Center bars the insignia from SI-generated imagery and prohibits phrases like "according to NASA" in SI products.

## Backyard Worlds context

- Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 (since 2017, led by Marc Kuchner) used unWISE flipbooks. About 200,000 volunteers. A 2026 paper reports 3,006 new motion-confirmed L and T candidates (arXiv 2604.01323).
- Its Zooniverse page now says "Finished! Looks like this project is out of data at the moment!" WISE ended July 31, 2024.
- Its "Think You've Got One" form has over 102,000 submissions, each inspected by the science team.
- Volunteer vetting steps:
  - A real mover appears in all frames. Ghosts appear in only some.
  - WiseView's Gaia overlay marks known Gaia sources.
  - Search SIMBAD within 1 arcmin.
  - Check VizieR for proper motion above about 100 mas/yr.
  - Don't trust AllWISE proper motions ("systematically high").
- Science team vetting: confirm motion by eye, photometric type L0 or later, motion above 3 sigma (CatWISE2020, Gaia DR3, or unTimely), then spectroscopy.
- Related projects: Cool Neighbors (2023), Binaries (June 2026), and "The Rogue Worlds Project ... with SPHEREx" (AMNH, selected by NASA Oct 28, 2026, no public tool yet).
- 299 Backyard Worlds discoveries already have SPHEREx spectra (https://zenodo.org/records/20603369). Gagné et al. 2026 extracted SPHEREx spectra for 6,003 ultracool dwarfs, 947 of them new.
